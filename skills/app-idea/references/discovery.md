# Discovery: the question ladders

Each ladder is a short sequence of questions ("rungs"). Climb one rung per
round per ladder, and only ask a rung once the rungs before it are
answered. Translate the wording into the person's language and keep the
meaning.

## Which ladders

Their answer to "where are you now" decides which ladders are required:

| Situation | Required ladders |
|---|---|
| Nothing yet, it's a new idea | problem, value, alternatives |
| People already do this somehow | problem, value, surprise |
| People already pay me for it | value, surprise, alternatives |
| It's for my own team | problem, value |

Add the **risk** ladder at the end only if an important cell (who, today,
smallest version) is still a guess.

## The rungs, in the words to use

**Problem ladder**
- **who**: "Name one person, or one role you could message today, who
  needs this most. What goes wrong for them when it doesn't exist?"
- **today**: "How do they handle it right now, even badly?"
- **cost**: "What does that cost them: time, money, mistakes, lost
  customers?" If the answer is "nothing", say plainly that this usually
  means the problem isn't painful enough yet, and ask what would make it
  painful.
- **trigger**: "When do they reach for this? What just happened?"

**Value ladder**
- **smallest version**: "If only one part worked next week, which part
  would <person> actually use?" (Ask it against today: "What one thing
  would make <person> use this instead of <what they do today>?") Push past
  "it all has to work together", because that usually means the most
  important part isn't clear yet.
- **signal**: "A week after it's live, how will you know it worked?" Offer
  three countable options based on the smallest version ("10 bookings
  through the page", "no double bookings", "half the regulars used it
  once") and let them type their own.
- **later**: "What would you add in a year? That waits for later." These
  answers go into "Not in this version", each with half a sentence on why.

**Surprise ladder**
- **surprised**: "When you watched someone do this, what did they do that
  you didn't expect?" Watching counts; surveys don't.
- **today** (confirm): "So today they… is that right?"

**Alternatives ladder**
- **alternatives**: "If this didn't exist, what would they use? What's good
  about that?" Write down the good part, because it is the bar to clear.
- **why not that**: "What would make them switch?"

**Risk ladder** (only when needed)
- **riskiest**: "Which of these guesses, if wrong, would sink it?" Use the
  cells that are still guesses as the options.
- **cheapest test**: "How could we check that before building, cheaply?"
  The answer is often not software: "ask five regulars on Zalo this week".

If the brief already answers a rung confidently, don't ask it again.
Confirm it inside another question instead ("You said regulars: is that
people who already know you?").

## The brief cells

| Cell | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Building (reframe) | One sentence: what they are actually building. Written last. |
| The problem | What goes wrong today, and for whom |
| Who | A person or role, never a category |
| Today | What they do instead right now |
| What it costs | Time, money, mistakes, missed customers |
| The moment | When they reach for it |
| Smallest useful version | The one part someone would use this week |
| How we'll know | A countable signal a week or a month after going live |
| Not in this version | What waits, each with half a sentence of why |
| Constraints | Languages, brand, dates, legal, "must run on my server", budget |
| Where you are | Nothing yet / people use something / people pay / own team |

**Confidence.** A cell they said in so many words is theirs. A cell you
inferred is a guess: prefix it with "I think —" so they can correct it.
Correcting a guess is the point of showing it.

**Before asking "Is this what you're building?"** the reframe, who, today
and smallest-version cells must be filled. If one is still a guess, ask
about it, or say clearly that it is a guess when you ask for confirmation.

## Teaching on the way

Every question carries one honest line on why it matters: a reason, not a
lecture. If they ask what a concept means, use these plain explanations:

- **The person:** "Apps built for everyone are built for no one. One real
  name makes every later choice easier."
- **What they do today:** "What people do today, even badly, is what your
  app has to beat. If they do nothing, the problem may not hurt enough
  yet."
- **The cost of today:** "Knowing what the problem costs tells us how much
  your app is worth to them, and which part matters most."
- **The smallest useful version:** "If only one part worked next week,
  which part would someone use? That part is the heart of the idea. Build
  it first and real people can try it sooner."
- **How we'll know it works:** "A number you can count after launch, so
  you know whether to keep going, change course, or stop."
- **An assumption:** "A guess we're building on. Writing it down means we
  can check it and change it later without surprises."
- **Not in this version:** "Things you want, saved for later on purpose.
  They aren't lost, they just don't slow down the first version."
- **Now, next, later:** "Now is what we build first. Next comes once the
  first version proves itself. Later is the bigger dream."
- **A one-way door:** "A decision that's hard to undo, like deleting data
  or charging cards. These always get your explicit yes."
- **A signal:** "Something countable that shows real people got value."
- **A story:** "One thing a real person will be able to do, written in
  their words, with how we'll check it works."
- **A direction (look):** "One clear visual style, chosen on purpose for
  your kind of business, so every screen feels like the same app."
